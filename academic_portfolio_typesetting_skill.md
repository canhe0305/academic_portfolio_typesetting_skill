# Academic / PhD Research Portfolio Content & Typesetting Skill

## Objective
Transform raw project materials into a **research-oriented portfolio** for PhD, MPhil/PhD, DDes, and practice-based research applications.

This skill is for **research portfolios**, not general taught-programme portfolios.

The portfolio must demonstrate:
- research direction
- methodological capacity
- project-to-proposal relevance
- personal contribution
- visual and textual evidence
- coherent future trajectory

---

## Scope
Use this skill for:
- PhD applications
- MPhil/PhD applications
- DDes and design research applications
- practice-based research applications
- research-oriented fellowships or residencies requiring a supporting portfolio

Do **not** use this as-is for:
- general MA/MFA visual portfolios
- commercial design portfolios
- brand-facing pitch decks
- exhibition catalogues

---

## Core Definition
An academic / PhD research portfolio is **not** a general image book.

It is a **structured evidence document** that supports a proposed research direction.

Each included project must help answer at least one of the following:
- How does this project support the proposed research question?
- What method does this project demonstrate?
- What problem does this project expose or investigate?
- How does this project connect past practice to future research?
- What role did the applicant personally play in producing this work?

If a project cannot answer any of these questions, it should be removed or downgraded.

---

## Required Portfolio Qualities
The portfolio must be:
- research-led, not display-led
- concise, not archival
- annotated, not image-dumped
- method-aware, not software-list-based
- visually restrained, not decorative
- selective, not comprehensive
- structurally clear, not narratively bloated

---

## Portfolio Architecture
Use the following macro-structure unless the application requires a different format.

### 1. Cover
Purpose:
- identify applicant
- establish research-facing tone

Required content:
- name
- portfolio type
- year

Rules:
- minimal
- no decorative collage
- no unnecessary visual effects

### 2. Research Positioning
Purpose:
- state the applicant's research direction

Required content:
- one short positioning paragraph
- 3-5 keywords, dimensions, or topic axes

Must clarify:
- research problem
- method orientation
- relevant material or field

Must not become:
- autobiography
- generic artist statement
- CV summary

### 3. Structure Map
Purpose:
- explain how the portfolio is organized

Allowed formats:
- cluster map
- timeline
- method map
- question map

Must show:
- relationship between selected projects and research direction

Must not be:
- simple contents list only

### 4. Evidence Clusters
Purpose:
- organize selected projects into coherent groups

Recommended cluster logic:
- by research theme
- by method
- by medium or scale
- by developmental stage

### 5. Closing Research Trajectory
Purpose:
- connect existing work to future research

Must clarify:
- what the next research step is
- why the applicant is ready to pursue it

Must not end with:
- generic thanks
- contact-only ending
- empty aspiration language

---

## Project Selection Rules

### Keep a project if it satisfies at least two of the following:
- highly relevant to proposed research
- demonstrates a distinct method
- reveals a long-term problem awareness
- can be understood quickly as a strong case
- shows that a key issue has already been encountered in practice
- belongs to recent work, especially after the current research direction emerged

### Remove or downgrade a project if it is:
- unrelated to the current research direction
- visually strong but conceptually irrelevant
- repetitive in method or form
- only skill-demonstrating, not inquiry-demonstrating
- collaborative with unclear personal contribution
- overly dependent on lengthy explanation without strong relevance

### Quantity Rules
- Prefer 3 strong projects over 7 weak ones
- Prefer 10 focused pages over 30 diffuse pages
- Prefer annotated evidence over full process dumps

---

## Page Types
Only use page types that serve a clear function.

### cover
Use for:
- title page only

Required:
- applicant identity
- no irrelevant imagery

### research-positioning
Use for:
- concise research framing

Required:
- 1 short paragraph
- 3-5 keywords or dimensions

### structure-map
Use for:
- explaining organizational logic

Required:
- explicit relation between portfolio contents and research question

### project-opener
Use for:
- opening a single project

Required:
- project title
- 1 framing sentence
- metadata block
- 1 hero image optional but preferred for practice-based work

Rule:
- framing sentence must state the research relevance of the project

### annotated-evidence
Use for:
- images, interface screenshots, diagrams, texts, installation views, charts, or material evidence

Required:
- 1-4 evidence units
- annotation for each evidence unit

Rule:
- annotations must explain research function, not merely describe the object

### method-page
Use for:
- method, system logic, experimental structure, workflow, or inquiry design

Rule:
- explain how method responds to the research problem
- do not turn into a production diary
- do not mistake software lists for method

### contribution-page
Use for:
- collaborative work only

Required when relevant:
- explicit personal role
- clear responsibility breakdown

Suggested categories:
- concept
- research
- writing
- prototyping
- interface
- installation
- technical execution

### comparison-page
Use for:
- comparing versions, cases, methods, conditions, or outcomes

Rule:
- comparison must have research meaning
- title must state the comparison dimension

### claim-page
Use for:
- stating the key argument of a project or cluster

Required:
- judgment-based title
- 2-4 supporting points
- optional evidence support

### cluster-transition
Use for:
- moving from one project group to another

Rule:
- explain why the next cluster matters
- do not use as a decorative separator only

### future-trajectory
Use for:
- ending the portfolio with forward direction

Required:
- next research questions, methods, or directions
- explicit continuity with the preceding portfolio

## Text Rules

### Titles
Titles must be analytical or judgment-based.

Preferred title types:
- problem statement
- methodological claim
- structural observation
- research judgment

Good examples:
- This project turns deletion into a visible but unverifiable governance interface.
- The system does not simulate intelligence; it tests how expression is pre-structured.
- What is shown here is not a result object, but the organization of judgment in a system.

Avoid:
- Project Overview
- Design Process
- Final Output
- Inspiration
- repeated project titles as section titles

### Body Text
Body text must be short and functional.

Each paragraph should do one of the following:
- define
- argue
- annotate
- explain
- connect to the research line

Body text must not become:
- generic background padding
- emotional reflection dump
- flattering application prose
- over-literary explanation

### Annotation
Annotation is preferred over long summary.

Each annotation should do at least one of the following:
- state research function
- state methodological role
- explain why a visual element matters
- locate an item within the project or cluster
- clarify personal contribution

## Visual and Layout System
The portfolio must look like a **research evidence document with designed readability**, not just a general portfolio layout.

It must not look like:
- a commercial pitch deck
- a luxury lookbook
- a training-school portfolio template
- a plain academic paper exported as PDF

### Grid
Preferred:
- 12-column grid

Fallback:
- 6-column grid

Rules:
- titles, body, annotations, metadata, and images must align to stable axes
- keep margins consistent throughout
- do not change layout language for every project

### Alignment
Preferred:
- left-aligned text blocks
- asymmetrical image placement allowed only when a clear relation to text is preserved

Forbidden:
- floating blocks without shared visual axes
- arbitrary center alignment across most pages

### Density
Target density by page type:
- cover / transition: 20-35%
- research-positioning / structure-map: 35-50%
- project-opener: 30-45%
- annotated-evidence / method-page / claim-page: 45-65%
- future-trajectory: 25-40%

Rules:
- do not make pages empty for the sake of elegance
- do not overfill pages to simulate productivity
- vary page weight across the document

### Spread Rhythm
When producing PDF portfolios, consider adjacent pages as paired rhythm units.

Recommended pairings:
- hero image page + claim page
- method page + evidence page
- transition page + new project opener

Do not make every page carry the same visual weight.

### Typography
Preferred fonts:
- Inter
- Helvetica or closely related neutral sans-serif families
- Noto Sans CJK / Source Han Sans / other neutral modern Chinese sans-serif

Typography rules:
- use hierarchy through size, weight, spacing, and block structure
- do not rely on decorative effects
- keep annotation smaller than body text, but always readable
- keep metadata visually subordinate

Minimum hierarchy:
- portfolio title
- cluster / section title
- project title
- framing / claim line
- body text
- annotation / caption
- metadata

### Image Hierarchy
Classify images into four levels.

#### Hero Image
Purpose:
- main viewing entry for a project

Rules:
- usually one per project
- do not fragment with too many surrounding small images

#### Evidence Image
Purpose:
- prove how the project exists
- show interface, installation, system, space, interaction, chart, or material evidence

Rules:
- annotate each one
- 1-4 evidence units per page maximum
- avoid repetitive angles

#### Detail Image
Purpose:
- show local mechanism, texture, screen detail, or material condition

Rules:
- only use with contextual support
- do not flood the portfolio with isolated detail shots

#### Context Image
Purpose:
- show exhibition environment, participant relation, spatial scale, or situational context

Rules:
- especially important for installations, systems, performance, and spatial works

### Annotation Visual Grammar
Annotation is a primary layer in research portfolios.

Rules:
- place annotation adjacent to the referenced image or evidence
- do not stack all notes at the page bottom
- use numbering, side labels, light connector lines, or compact caption blocks if needed
- annotation must remain restrained and readable
- do not make annotation look like engineering schematics or museum wall labels

### Metadata
Maintain a stable metadata system.

Recommended metadata fields:
- project title
- year
- medium / format
- project type
- personal role if collaborative
- optional keywords

Rules:
- place metadata in a consistent position across projects
- treat metadata as a support layer, not a main visual layer

### Color
Default palette:
- black, white, gray
- maximum one low-saturation accent color

Accent color may be used for:
- cluster differentiation
- claim emphasis
- annotation numbering
- key research keywords

Forbidden:
- high-saturation backgrounds
- decorative gradients
- excessive blue “tech” styling
- using color instead of hierarchy

### Chinese and Bilingual Typesetting
For Chinese:
- use higher line-height than English body text
- avoid overly narrow columns
- avoid strong negative tracking

For bilingual layouts:
- preserve technical and proper terms in original language when needed
- keep spacing and punctuation consistent
- do not mirror-translate every paragraph line by line
- use one primary reading language and one supporting layer only

---

## Recommended Templates

### Project Opener Template
Must contain:
- project title
- 1 framing / claim sentence
- metadata block
- optional hero image

### Annotated Evidence Template
Must contain:
- 1-3 evidence images or units
- annotation adjacent to each
- optional compact caption or keyword block

### Method + Claim Template
Must contain:
- method structure on one side
- research claim on the other side

### Comparison Template
Must contain:
- two aligned conditions or cases
- stated comparison dimension
- annotation or analytical label on both sides

### Future Trajectory Template
Must contain:
- 1 short closing paragraph
- 2-4 future directions, questions, or methods
- optional low-weight supporting image

---

## Hard Constraints
- each page serves one primary function only
- each page has one main title only
- every included project must relate to the research line
- every collaborative project must specify personal contribution
- do not turn the portfolio into a general visual booklet
- do not paste the proposal directly into the portfolio
- do not use long archival project dumps
- do not use image quantity to simulate strength
- do not discuss form without research relevance
- do not leave images unannotated when they function as evidence

---

## Failure Conditions
The output fails if any of the following occurs:
- it looks like a standard MFA or MA visual portfolio rather than an academic / PhD research portfolio
- the relation to the proposed research is unclear
- there are many projects but no visible research line
- there are many images but no claims or annotations
- there is too much text but no structure
- collaborative roles are unclear
- the portfolio tries to replace the proposal instead of supporting it
- the document is too long, too full, or too diffuse
- it adopts a brand deck, luxury lookbook, or training-template aesthetic
- it uses decorative styling that overwhelms evidence and reading order

---

## Workflow
1. identify the proposed research direction
2. extract relevant projects only
3. rewrite each selected project in terms of research relevance
4. select the strongest evidence for each project
5. add annotation and contribution information
6. group projects into coherent clusters
7. connect the clusters to future research trajectory
8. remove redundant pages, images, and explanations
9. verify visual hierarchy, density, and annotation placement
10. run final QA

---

## Final QA Checklist
Before output, confirm all of the following:
- the research direction is explicit
- every project clearly supports that direction
- the portfolio is selective rather than comprehensive
- annotations explain why evidence matters
- personal contribution is explicit where needed
- titles are analytical rather than generic
- layout is restrained, legible, and structurally consistent
- images function as evidence rather than decoration
- the ending points toward future research clearly
- the full document is concise, coherent, and application-ready

---

## Output Standard
The final portfolio must be:
- research-oriented
- concise
- evidence-based
- visually restrained
- annotation-led
- method-aware
- structurally coherent
- suitable for academic / PhD research portfolio generation for PhD, MPhil/PhD, DDes, and practice-based research applications
